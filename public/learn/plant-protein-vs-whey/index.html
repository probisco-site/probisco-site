Plant Protein vs Whey: What Is the Difference?

Whey comes from milk; plant proteins come from soya, peas, nuts and grains. How they differ in source, digestion, allergens and diet fit.

By the ProBisCo team · 18 November 2026

"Plant protein or whey?" is one of the most common questions people ask when they start paying attention to protein. Both can be part of a good diet. The difference is mostly about where the protein comes from and what else comes with it.

What whey is

Whey is the liquid left behind when milk is turned into cheese or paneer. It is dried and filtered into powders. Whey concentrate still contains some milk sugar (lactose) and fat; whey isolate is filtered further and has less of both. Whey is a dairy product, so it suits vegetarians who eat dairy, but not vegans or people with a milk allergy.

What plant protein is

Plant protein comes from foods such as soya, peas, lentils, nuts and grains. Soya protein isolate, the main protein in ProBisCo, is made from soybeans and contains all nine essential amino acids, which makes it one of the most complete plant proteins. Almond flour and oats add smaller amounts of protein along with flavour and texture.

Which is better?

For most people eating a varied diet, the total amount of protein across the day matters more than whether one snack used whey or soya. Choose based on your diet, your tolerance for dairy, any allergies and, honestly, which products you enjoy eating.

As a reference point, the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR-NIN) recommends about 0.83g of protein per kg of body weight per day for healthy adults (source). For a 60 kg adult that is roughly 50g a day. Athletes and people with specific health needs may need different amounts; a doctor or dietitian can advise.
