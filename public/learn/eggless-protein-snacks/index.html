Eggless Protein Snacks for Vegetarians: A Practical Guide

Simple eggless, vegetarian ways to add protein to your snacks in India, from roasted chana and paneer to protein cookies, and what to check on packs.

By the ProBisCo team · 18 November 2026

Many vegetarians in India don't eat eggs, and a lot of "high-protein" snacks rely on eggs or meat. The good news is that there are plenty of eggless options. Some come from your kitchen, and some come in a packet.

From the kitchen

  • Roasted chana and peanuts. Cheap, crunchy and easy to carry.
  • Paneer and curd. Paneer tikka, a bowl of curd or hung-curd dips.
  • Sprouts chaat. Moong or mixed sprouts with onion, tomato and lemon.
  • Besan and dal snacks. Besan chilla, dhokla or a moong dal cheela.
  • Nuts and seeds. Almonds, pumpkin seeds and flax add protein along with healthy fats.

From a packet

Packaged snacks are handy at work, in a gym bag or on the road. When you pick one, check:

  • The veg mark and the ingredients. The green dot tells you it is vegetarian; the ingredient list tells you whether it contains egg. "Eggless" should be clear on the pack.
  • Protein per serving. A few grams more than a regular biscuit adds up over a week. See how to read protein on a label.
  • Protein source. Whey and milk solids come from dairy; soya, pea and nut proteins come from plants. Our guide on plant protein vs whey explains the difference.
  • Sugar and calories. A snack is still a snack. Know what you are eating.

Make it a habit you'll keep

The best protein snack is the one you actually reach for instead of a regular biscuit or namkeen. Keep one at your desk, one in your bag and one in the kitchen. Pair a crunchy snack with chai or coffee rather than eating it on the run.
